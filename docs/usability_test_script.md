# Usability Test Script — D17

5 participants, about 15 minutes each. Students in the same programme who have
not seen the app before. Test on a laptop, app already running on Home.

## What to say first

"This is a study assistant for our course. I want to see whether it makes sense
to someone using it for the first time. I'm testing the app, not you — anything
confusing is the app's fault, not yours. Please think out loud: say what you're
looking at, what you expect to happen, and what you're trying to do. I won't
answer questions while you work, so I can see where you get stuck. Stop whenever
you want."

Ask permission before taking notes. Do not demo anything first.

## Tasks

Hand these over one at a time. Start a timer when they begin, stop when they say
they're done or give up. Do not help, even when it hurts to watch.

**Task 1 — Find a module.**
"Find the study material for Module 2 of Artificial Intelligence."
Done when: Module Assistant is open on AI201-M2.
Target: under 60 seconds.

**Task 2 — Ask about a rule.**
"Find out what the minimum attendance requirement is."
Done when: an answer from the regulations is on screen.
Target: under 90 seconds.
Watch for: do they notice the Syllabus / Regulation switch, or ask in the wrong mode?

**Task 3 — Take a quiz.**
"Test yourself on that module and find out your score."
Done when: a quiz is submitted and the score is showing.
Target: under 2 minutes.

**Task 4 — Read the network.**
"Using the Knowledge Network, tell me what you need to understand before you can
learn A Star Search."
Done when: they name the prerequisites.
Target: under 90 seconds.
Watch for: do they read the arrow direction correctly? Do they find the zoom buttons?

**Task 5 — Check a source.**
"How do you know that attendance answer is correct?"
Done when: they open the sources under the answer.
Target: under 45 seconds.
This tests whether the citations are discoverable at all.

## While they work, write down

- Where their cursor goes first on each task
- Anything they say out loud that starts with "I thought", "where is", "can I"
- Wrong turns: which page they opened by mistake
- Anything they never noticed (mode switch, Sources, zoom buttons, module picker)
- Where they looked for help

## Afterwards, ask

1. What was the most confusing part?
2. Was anything not where you expected it?
3. Would you trust an answer from this before an exam? Why?
4. What would you add or remove?

## Rules for the facilitator

- Say "what would you do if I wasn't here?" instead of answering.
- Silence is data. Let it run.
- Never say "just click there".
- If they are stuck past 3 minutes, mark it failed and move on.
